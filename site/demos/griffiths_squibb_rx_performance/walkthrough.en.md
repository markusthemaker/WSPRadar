# #3 Prof. Griffiths (G3ZIL) et al. RX Performance: A Month on 40 m with a Low Dipole

<!-- section: setup -->
## Reproduce this setup

Select ‘Run in WSPRadar App’ to load this demo and start its analysis.

<!-- section: step-1 -->
## Change the question from comparison to reception

This is **RX Performance** for G3ZIL on 40 m in April 2017. There is no reference receiver and no G3ZIL − G4HZX comparison. Ask where G3ZIL heard transmitters, how consistently it decoded confirmed opportunities, and how the successful SNR varied. Opportunities are conditional on evidence that G3ZIL was receiving; this is not a station-uptime measurement.

<!-- section: step-2 -->
## Separate reach from Decode Rate

Start in **Map View**. Hearing a transmitter at least once contributes to reach; decoding a large proportion of its confirmed opportunities describes consistency. Map colors show station-balanced Decode Rate, giving each qualifying transmitter identity equal weight. In **Segment Inspector → Performance Evidence**, read that rate alongside the opportunity-level rate and the station and opportunity counts. The two rates answer differently weighted questions.

<!-- section: step-3 -->
## Follow the selected EA4URA path

In **Station Insights**, retain the preselected **EA4URA (IN80CI)** identity. Compare its evidence by UTC hour with its chronological evidence: the first combines the same hours across the month; the second shows differences between dates. Use the documented early-morning and evening examples as reading landmarks, then inspect the counts supporting each part of the pattern.

<!-- section: step-4 -->
## Keep successful SNR and reception consistency together

SNR charts contain successful decodes only. A strong received signal therefore does not, on its own, establish reliable reception. **Successful RX SNR Deviation** compares each path with its own median successful SNR; zero is that path's typical level. Read the temporal SNR pattern with Decode Rate and evidence coverage. Changing propagation, local noise, and other station effects may all contribute.
