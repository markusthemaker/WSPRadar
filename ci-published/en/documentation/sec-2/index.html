1. Choose and Prepare the Analysis

Start with one station question. State what you changed or want to investigate, and which observation would help answer it.

1.1 Prepare your station and reports

  1. Create usable reports. For RX, configure a WSPR-capable receiver and decoder, such as WSJT-X, with the correct receiving callsign, locator, band, audio input and synchronized clock. Enable uploads: a local decode that was never uploaded is unavailable to WSPRadar. For TX, configure the callsign, locator, band, timing and truthful transmit-power report; remote receivers provide the spots. The WSJT-X reference covers basic setup.
  2. Check your callsign and locator in the database. Check the exact RX reporting callsign or TX callsign, reported locator and UTC times in WSPRnet, then in the database WSPRadar will use. Use the callsign exactly as recorded, including any suffix. Choose a completed period and allow reports to arrive; no fixed delay guarantees completeness. See data troubleshooting.
  3. Keep the setup interpretable. Record the antenna, feedline, radio, decoder, gain or power settings, software, schedule and deliberate changes. Keep other variables stable and clocks synchronized. For Benchmark, prepare both setups using RX guidance or TX guidance before collecting the comparison.

1.2 Configure your analysis

Choose the analysis from Section 0.2. Guided leads you through the settings; Compact presents the same analysis choices in a shorter form.

Enter the exact Target callsign, one band, and a completed UTC window. Enter Target Grid Locator (optional) as a four- or six-character Maidenhead locator, or leave it blank to discover the location after Run analysis. One reported locator is selected automatically; several require your choice, preserving all four or six characters. The first four characters select reports, while the full entered or discovered locator anchors the map, distances and directions. Longer windows add evidence but can mix operating and propagation conditions.

For Benchmark, enter the Reference callsign exactly as reported; it must differ from Target. Leave Reference Grid Locator (optional) blank to find the location from reports after Run analysis, or enter its independently known four-character locator. If several locations appear, choose one. A location alone does not establish operation or a shared physical site.

Confirm the operating conditions independently:

  • RX Benchmark: both receivers listened on the selected band with functioning uploads throughout the period. This does not require a decode in every cycle.
  • TX Benchmark: both transmitters sent in the same WSPR cycles, with aligned schedules and message patterns.

Keep the operating records that support this. WSPRadar cannot establish continuous operation from successful reports alone. Before you run a Benchmark, go to RUN ANALYSIS in Review and select I have confirmed common operation, or accept the limitations when it is unverified. This includes the maintained Milazzo example and other historical reports without independently verified common operation. The acceptance does not record which alternative applies and is not proof of operation; see Section 10.3.

Leave Reference SNR correction at No correction unless you have an independently established offset valid for this setup. See calibration guidance.

For the first run, review the displayed filters and evidence requirements without lowering them merely to obtain more colored map areas. Maximum peer distance from Target (km) changes which remote stations enter the analysis, not just the map zoom; choose it to match your question. See scope and evidence controls. Check the final summary, then select Run RX Analysis or Run TX Analysis. Exact controls are in Chapter 10; unexpected gaps are addressed in Chapter 11.

1.3 Navigate the results

Follow one pattern through the results. Open How to read this beside a view when you need its legend, counting rules or interpretation.

In Performance, At a glance above Global View summarizes the whole completed analysis: decoding frequency, station reach, the mean of the stations' individual decode rates and the distances of decoded signals. Section 8.4.2 explains which stations enter these summaries and how the rates and distances are weighted.

1. Global View — where is the pattern? A map segment covers directions and distances from Target. Read its numerical scale and supporting counts. Gray diagonal hatching (Not qualifying) means observations remain for this map but its coloring requirements are unmet. An unfilled segment (No observations) has none remaining. Performance uses stations meeting its station requirements; Benchmark retains all observed stations within the selected area and filters. A zero value stays colored when requirements are met. Hatching and unfilled areas do not mean a measured zero reception rate.

2. Segment Inspector — how broad and persistent is it? Select directions and distances. This active scope determines the stations and observations in Decodes & Stations, population counts, segment statistics and time plots. Decodes & Stations follows the Active scope summary; its count cards precede Benchmark Statistics. Compare station and observation counts, then check individual dates before calling a combined hour-of-day pattern recurring. In Benchmark Statistics, compare the spot-pair, station-mean and station-median ΔSNR distributions. Frequently observed stations supply more spot pairs; each included station contributes one mean or median to the station distributions.

3. Station Insights → Selected Station Evidence — which stations explain it? Select a typical station, an unusual one, or one with many reports. Both Performance and Benchmark list the whole completed analysis by default. Filter by selected segment and distance narrows the list; station metrics and plots still cover the full run, even if the filter hides the selected station. This list filter lasts only for the session. In Benchmark, Include one-sided evidence also shows stations without spot pairs and one-sided Drill-Down observations.

4. Drill-Down — what happened in individual cycles? Check exact callsigns and locators, UTC times, SNR and outcomes. Focus the time window to inspect an interval. These observations have already been matched and filtered; they are not an untouched database download.

Reading the statistical summaries. The charts use the following terms:

Term Meaning
Arithmetic mean (Mean) Add the values and divide by their count.
Median The middle value after sorting; with an even count, average the two middle values. Mean and Median summarize the same observations but respond differently to extreme values.
Confidence interval (CI) Describes uncertainty in an estimated mean under the stated sampling model.
Sample standard deviation (SD) Describes how widely the observed values vary.
Standard error Describes the model-based uncertainty of the mean. Under the independent-observation model, it is sample SD divided by the square root of the observation count.
Interquartile range (IQR) Q3 minus Q1. The plotted Q1–Q3 interval covers the middle 50% of the values: it shows their spread, not uncertainty of the mean.

A time bin groups observations in one displayed interval. Its pointwise confidence interval, labelled Bin 95% CI, applies to that bin's mean, not simultaneously to the whole curve. The percentage follows the selected confidence level. That level describes the procedure over repeated samples, not a probability assigned to this particular interval. Section 8.5.1 explains the calculation and independence assumptions.
